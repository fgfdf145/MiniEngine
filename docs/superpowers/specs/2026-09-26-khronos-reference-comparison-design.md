# Khronos Reference Comparison

## Goal

Accept material work against the Khronos implementation, not only by eye: render Khronos'
`glTF-Sample-Assets` test models in the engine and in the Khronos glTF Sample Viewer under the same
conditions, and put the two side by side. Agreed with the user on 2026-09-26, after phase 3; every
later material phase is accepted this way too.

## What the Sample Viewer Does

Read from the release build at `https://github.khronos.org/glTF-Sample-Viewer-Release/`
(`GltfSVApp.js`), 2026-09-26:

- Environment `Cannon_Exterior` (the `low_resolution_hdrs` branch of `glTF-Sample-Environments`),
  environment rotation 90 degrees, IBL intensity 1, background blurred. Punctual lights from the
  glTF only; no default lights while IBL is on.
- Tone map `KHR_PBR_NEUTRAL`, exposure 1.0 (a linear multiplier): an environment texel of 1 reaches the
  tone mapper as 1.
- Camera: vertical FOV 45 degrees, target at the centre of the scene's extents, looking down -Z;
  distance `max(xZoom, yZoom)` with `zoom = max(width, height) / 2 / tan(fov / 2)` over the extents'
  x and y sizes and each axis' FOV (the horizontal one taken as the vertical times the aspect ratio).
  The extents (`getSceneExtents`) are the union of every primitive's box grown to the cube around its
  bounding sphere: for a sphere, sqrt(3) times its radius on every axis.
- `?model=<url>` loads a model; environment, tone map and material variant are chosen in its UI.

## Decisions

1. **A Khronos reference view in the engine** (`RenderDebugSettings::khronosReference`, a Graphics
   Debug checkbox, and `--khronos-reference` on the command line). While on:
   - tone mapping is Khronos PBR Neutral (the published reference implementation, in
     `pbr_neutral.glsl`) instead of GT7's, applied to the exposed value (`hdr * kExposedPerFrameBufferUnit`);
   - the output is encoded as the viewer encodes it, with a 2.2 gamma rather than the sRGB curve
     (`KhronosViewerOutputForSrgbTarget`, the value the sRGB target turns into `pow(x, 1/2.2)`; SDR
     only; added 2026-09-26);
   - exposure is manual, set so the scene's HDRI texel value 1 is exposed to 1 (the viewer's
     exposure 1.0); auto exposure and auto white balance are off;
   - glare/bloom, screen-space AO, SSR and geometric specular AA are off: the viewer has none of them
     (specular AA widens the roughness of small curved surfaces, added 2026-09-26). TAA stays (the
     viewer uses MSAA; both converge on the same edges);
   - the camera is framed by the viewer's rule above over every model in the scene
     (`ComputeKhronosViewerExtents` from each submesh's cached bounding sphere,
     `Camera::FrameBoundsLikeKhronosViewer`), again whenever those extents or the viewport's aspect
     ratio change, so a scene that finishes loading is reframed; in between the camera is the user's;
   - an HDRI background shows the prefiltered environment at roughness 0.6, the viewer's blur.
   Off, nothing changes: GT7's curve and every effect as before.
2. **The models** are fetched by `tools/khronos_reference/fetch.py` from `KhronosGroup/glTF-Sample-Assets`
   (the `glTF` flavour of each model) and `Cannon_Exterior.hdr` from `glTF-Sample-Environments`, into
   `assets/khronos/` (not version controlled, as all of `assets/`). The model list lives in the
   script; the first set covers what the engine supports today: `Compare*` for base colour, metallic,
   roughness, normal, clearcoat, sheen, specular, IOR, iridescence, anisotropy and emissive strength,
   plus `ClearCoatTest`, `SheenTestGrid`, `SpecularTest`, `IORTestGrid`, `AnisotropyStrengthTest`,
   `AnisotropyRotationTest`, `IridescenceMetallicSpheres`, `IridescenceDielectricSpheres`,
   `TextureTransformTest`, `UnlitTest`, `EmissiveStrengthTest` and `MaterialsVariantsShoe`. Phase 4
   adds the transmission models when it starts.
3. **Scenes** are generated by `tools/khronos_reference/make_scenes.py` into `assets/scenes/khronos/`:
   the model at the origin, the HDRI environment turned to match the viewer's 90 degrees (the
   engine's 180, found by sweeping the rotation against `CompareMetallic`), no lights. One scene per
   variant for a model with variants. The HDRI path is absolute: scene HDRI paths are not resolved
   against the assets folder.
4. **Engine captures**: `tools/khronos_reference/capture_engine.sh` runs each scene with
   `--khronos-reference --capture` into `assets/khronos/captures/engine/`.
5. **Viewer captures** are taken in a browser. `capture_server.py` serves the viewer on
   `127.0.0.1`, proxied from the release site (a browser may refuse a post from the public site to a
   local address), with a script injected that keeps the WebGL drawing buffer and defines
   `captureViewer(name)`, which scales the canvas to the engine capture's size and posts it back. The
   viewer is opened with `?model=` pointing at the same model on `raw.githubusercontent.com`, at the
   engine capture's size; variants are picked in its UI. The README gives the procedure.
6. **The comparison page**: `tools/khronos_reference/compare.py` (pure Python; no imaging library here)
   writes one HTML page with, per
   model, the viewer image, the engine image and a difference image, and a mean-difference number
   per model to watch between changes.

## Automated Verification

- PBR Neutral against values computed from Khronos' reference formula: black, mid-grey below the
  start of compression (unchanged but for the offset), a bright colour (compressed and desaturated),
  and the curve reaching `1 - startCompression` at the start of the shoulder.
- The viewer framing rule: a unit cube, a wide box (x decides), a tall box in a narrow view (the
  horizontal FOV decides); the extents of two spheres side by side, and of a placed entity.
- Exposure: the EV the reference view picks exposes an HDRI texel of 1 to 1 for several HDRI
  intensities.

## Manual Acceptance (by image)

1. `CompareBaseColor`, `CompareMetallic` and `CompareRoughness` are framed alike and lit alike
   (the environment's bright side on the same side) in both columns; brightness within a few
   percent on the grey patches.
2. Every model in the list loads and renders in the engine; the differences seen are listed in the
   feature documentation as known gaps or fixed.

## Results (2026-09-26)

Mean absolute difference over RGB, 0 to 255, 26 scenes. At the noise floor (2.7 to 4.6): base
colour, metallic, roughness, normal, clearcoat (both), sheen (both), specular (both), iridescence
(all three), anisotropy strength and rotation, emissive strength (both) and the shoe with each
variant. Known differences:

- `CompareIor` 14.5, `IORTestGrid` 12.3: glass through `KHR_materials_transmission`, phase 4.
- `CompareAnisotropy` 6.1: the spun face's bright and dark sectors turned the wrong way. Root cause:
  tangents generated for meshes without TANGENT put the bitangent along dP/dv, the image's bottom,
  where glTF's +Y is its top; normal maps were flipped in Y too (`CompareNormal`'s raised weave
  rendered engraved, which the mean difference barely showed). Fixed in 273a66f: CompareAnisotropy
  3.8, CompareNormal 2.6.
- `TextureTransformTest` 5.6: the engine ignored glTF samplers and repeated where the model asks for
  CLAMP_TO_EDGE. Fixed by `2026-09-26-gltf-samplers-design.md`: 2.7.
- `UnlitTest` 5.8: the viewer shows unlit colours without tone mapping; the engine tone maps them on
  purpose (see the unlit design).

Later on 2026-09-26, over 40 scenes: turning off specular AA in the reference view (the viewer has
none) kept every mean difference or lowered it by up to 0.3; encoding the output with the viewer's
2.2 gamma lowered 30 of 40 (TextureTransformTest 2.8 to 2.0, ClearCoatTest 4.2 to 3.2, most now 2.5
to 3.2). IORTestGrid's black spheres with specular 0.25 had looked far darker in the engine; their
reflections now match the viewer's within 1% (they did within 2% before, decoded by each image's own
curve). Its mean stays 5.8: the engine's roughness-0 reflections and refraction are softer than the
viewer's. UnlitTest rose from 5.9 to 6.4, since the viewer does not tone map unlit colours.

Two causes found on the way and fixed: emissive colours are cd/m^2 in the engine and plain units in
the viewer, so the scenes light the HDRI at 1 cd/m^2 per unit (ClearCoatTest's labels went from
black to 4.6); and the viewer measures each primitive's accessor box through its node, which grows
under a rotated node (CompareEmissiveStrength 10.7 to 3.7).
